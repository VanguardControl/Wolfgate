# SectorTravel

Splits the region into sector maps, each kept small enough to avoid floating-point error, joined only by jump beacons.
To cross, a pilot inside a beacon's zone hails the beacon platform. Its traffic control answers "CONFIRMED. FTL SEQUENCE
INITIATED.", and 10 seconds later the beacon throws a lightning bolt that opens a tilted bluespace portal as wide as the
ship across its nose. The moment any part of the ship touches the portal, it jumps to the linked sector. The portal
stays where it opened. No FTL drive and no fuel are needed.
Leaving the zone or taking hull damage during the countdown breaks it, an uncrossed portal closes after a minute, and an
arrival can't beacon-jump again for two minutes. Normal FTL never leaves its own sector. NPC freight runs can end in a
linked sector; any NPC crew whose dock, escort or follow target is over there queues a Cross order and goes through a
beacon too, without hailing. A faction holding a beacon's cell closes the link, which blockades every station in a sector
cut off from home; raiders named on a beacon move on its cell from time to time. Crates sold in another sector than they
were bought in pay a bonus per link. Admins use `wf_region`.

Entry points: `WFSectorTravelSystem` (round setup, `SectorOf`, `TryTransit`, region state, raids, NPC crossings,
haul bonus), `WFJumpBeaconSystem` (zones, hail answers, countdowns, bolt and portal, lockout), the `wfSector` prototypes
in `Resources/Prototypes/_WF/SectorTravel/sectors.yml`, and on the client the sector map window's Region view. The
beacon answers through the Hailing module as an automated end on a hidden console (`WFJumpBeaconConsole`).
