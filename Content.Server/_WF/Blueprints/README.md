# Blueprints

The Blueprint Artifact System replaces points research: crews hunt lost patterns at planet sites and bring them home
to unlock them. It is the R&D console (`ComputerResearchAndDevelopment`), so every ship with one has it and no map
changes are needed. The console opens the Codex window: a Codex tab with the server's technology tree, a Relics tab
with lost items outside it, a detail pane and the hunt bar. Pick up to four patterns of one tier and pay the fee (5,000,
15,000 or 40,000 by tier, from the server's research points first and the requester's bank for the rest). The locator claims a free planet site of that tier (I Moderate or easier, II
Hazardous, III Extreme), reveals its signal and hides one pattern shard each in the site's crates and lockers, or on its
floor. Shards are ordinary items, so anyone can take them. Use a shard on any powered R&D console to decode it into a
Blueprint Construct (10, 25 or 45 s by tier), then use the Construct on an R&D console to add the pattern's recipes to
that console's research server, printed at the protolathe, for the round.

A hunt ends when every shard has been decoded or destroyed, or when it is abandoned (shards still at the site are
removed, carried ones stay). The site is then released and the locator recalibrates for five minutes. Expeditions and
hunts never share a site (`WFPlanetPoiSite.BlueprintClaim`).

In the Codex tab green nodes are known, amber ones can be hunted, dull amber ones are being hunted and red ones need
their prerequisites first; a technology can't be located until its prerequisites are known. The Relics tab lists relics
by tier with a search box, tier filter and a "hide known" switch. The detail pane shows what a node or relic unlocks and
what its recipes cost, and the bar holds the picked patterns as chips, the fee and the Locate button, or the running
hunt and its Abandon button. The header shows the server, its research points, decoding arrays and how much of each
discipline is known.

The 46 patterns are items nothing else in the game hands out: entities with no lathe recipe, store, loot table or
loadout, judged one by one (a few of upstream's unplaced items, Goobstation's cell lasers and autosurgeon implants,
Mono's faction suits and ship gear, Frontier's leftovers). Their recipes print at the protolathe under the Patterns
category. Not included on purpose: antagonist kit, admin items, hull-breaching bombs and infinite ship ammo.

BAS replaces points research. Every tier-1 technology of a server's disciplines is unlocked free when the server
map-initialises (`ResearchSystem.WFGrantBasicTechnologies`). Every other technology is hunt-only: `techweb.yml`,
generated by `Tools/_WF/Blueprints/gen_techweb_patterns.py`, gives each one a pattern (tier 2 is Recovered; tier 3 is
Lost below 40,000 points and Forgotten at or above), `SharedResearchSystem.WFIsHuntOnly` keeps points from unlocking it
and the console shows it as unavailable. A Construct of such a pattern unlocks the whole technology. The locator only
lists a technology's pattern on a server that has one of its disciplines, so faction nodes are hunted from that
faction's servers; their Constructs can still be traded. Rerun the generator after techweb changes; `WFBlueprintTest`
fails while a technology has no pattern.

Research points now only pay locate fees. Passive sources earn nothing (`ResearchSystem.WFPassivePoints` is off), so
servers no longer tick and point disks can't be farmed at Sevastopol. Active science still earns points: artifact
analysis, anomaly vessels, xenobiology bounties and research disks found as loot. Research datafarms are decoding arrays
now: each powered one linked to the server cuts a quarter off the remaining decode time, down to 40%
(`WFBlueprintDecode.Scale`). Technology descriptions, such as the guidebook's, show the hunt tier instead of a point
cost, and the guidebook's Science and Technologies entries point at `Resources/ServerInfo/_WF/Blueprints`.

Entry points: `WFBlueprintSystem` (locator UI, decoding, unlock) and `WFBlueprintSystem.Hunts` (site choice, shard
placement, hunt end). Patterns are `wfBlueprintPattern` prototypes in `Resources/Prototypes/_WF/Blueprints`, with their
recipes in the protolathe's `WFBlueprintPatterns` pack. The full design and later phases are in
`Docs/_WF/Blueprints/DESIGN.md`.
