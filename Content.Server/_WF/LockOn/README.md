# LockOn

One firing solution per ship, shared by the helm and every gunnery console aboard. Middle-click a radar contact to
lock it; a gunnery console with no weapon selected also locks on a plain left click, so left click keeps firing when
guns are picked. Free entities on the clicked tile take priority over the grid beneath them, so rocks, debris and
loose items lock directly; anything on a grid locks that grid, so a ship or asteroid locks as one target. The
ship's own hull and everything aboard it are never valid targets. Clicking the same target again, or empty space,
clears the lock.

A lock draws a bracket and the target's name on every console's radar, and a lead cross for each selected gun. The
server measures the clicked hull point and its velocity, including the target grid's own spin, and replicates both
with a timestamp, so every console aboard solves the same shot instead of trusting its own view of someone else's
grid. Each cross accounts for the target's motion, the gun's projectile speed, and the turn a shot inherits from the
firing ship, so a barrel aimed at the cross meets a moving target while the hull is slewing. Missiles fired by any
gunnery console aboard while the ship holds a lock are pointed at that target before their first guidance tick, so
seeker and proportional-navigation missiles fly it without the gunner having to lead or the seeker picking another
contact.

`wf.lockon.debug_lead` (client, off by default) prints the solved flight time, target and muzzle speeds, gun speed
and lead distance beside each cross.

The ship being locked is warned once per `WFLockOnSystem.AlertCooldown` (10 seconds) through its PA, or in its
crew's chat when it has no working speakers, with a placeholder alarm sound. `WFLockOnAlertEvent` carries the warning
for other modules to react to.

Entry points: `WFLockOnSystem` (requests, validity, alerts), `WFLockOnSystem.Targeting.cs` (click resolution),
`WFLockOnSystem.Fire.cs` (missile hand-off), `WFLockOnComponent` (the ship's shared lock) and the client
`WFLockOnOverlay`.

Tuning lives on `WFLockOnComponent.MaxRange` (2560 m by default; a held lock clears past 1.25 times it).
